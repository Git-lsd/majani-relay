# Data card

This page lists every dataset Majani Relay learns from or is tested on: where it comes from, its licence, its size, what we use it for, and **what it does not cover**. Counts after our cleaning come from `results/` (generated by the code).

**What the data does well.** Kenyan arabica lesions (JMuBEN) and Brazilian whole leaves (BRACOL) cover all five leaf classes; RoCoLe adds leaves on the plant photographed with a smartphone in real field light, which the shipped model trains on and is cross-validated on; the Ugandan set is an East African farm test the model never trained on. Every dataset is open, licensed for reuse, and credited.

All five image datasets are open under **CC BY 4.0**: they may be shared and changed with credit. None of them was collected by us.

## 1. JMuBEN (Kenya, arabica: rust, Cercospora, Phoma)

- **Source:** https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/t2r6rszp5c/1 (DOI 10.17632/t2r6rszp5c.1)
- **Citation:** Jepkoech J., Kenduiywo B., Mugo D., Chebet E. (2021). *JMuBEN*. Mendeley Data, V1. Paper: Jepkoech J., Mugo D. M., Kenduiywo B. K., Too E. C. (2021). Arabica coffee leaf images dataset for coffee leaf disease detection and classification. *Data in Brief* 36, 107142. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2021.107142
- **Institutions:** University of Embu, Jomo Kenyatta University of Agriculture and Technology, Chuka University.
- **Licence:** CC BY 4.0.
- **Raw size:** three zip files, 549 MB in total (Cercospora 252 MB, leaf rust 69 MB, Phoma 227 MB). The paper lists 7,682 Cercospora, 8,337 rust and 6,572 Phoma images (22,591).
- **Conditions:** Mutira coffee plantation, Kirinyaga County, Kenya. The paper says "real-world conditions", one digital camera, labelled with the help of a plant pathologist. The images were cropped to the diseased area and resized. Smaller classes were enlarged with augmented copies (rotations and flips) of the same photos.
- **Role:** training data for the classes rust, Cercospora (brown eye spot) and Phoma. The main session sampled up to 1,500 photos per class. Used for training: rust 1,279, Cercospora 1,286, Phoma 1,275 (another 214–225 per class held out; these overlap with training through augmented copies, so they are not used as a headline).
- **What it does not cover:**
  - Whole leaves on the tree, as a relay farmer would photograph them. The crops show the lesion, not the leaf, the branch or the background.
  - Independent photos: many images are rotated or flipped copies of each other. A random train/test split puts copies of the same leaf on both sides, so accuracy measured inside JMuBEN is too optimistic. We do not use it as a headline number.
  - More than one camera, or phone cameras.
  - Healthy leaves and leaf miner (those are in JMuBEN2).
  - Coffee variety and season: not stated per image.
  - Coffee berry disease, bacterial blight, Fusarium, nutrient shortage, berries.

## 2. JMuBEN2 (Kenya, arabica: healthy, leaf miner)

- **Source:** https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/tgv3zb82nd/1 (DOI 10.17632/tgv3zb82nd.1)
- **Citation:** Jepkoech J., Mugo D., Kenduiywo B., Chebet E. (2021). *JMuBEN2*. Mendeley Data, V1. Same paper as JMuBEN.
- **Licence:** CC BY 4.0.
- **Raw size:** two zip files, 1.29 GB in total (healthy 567 MB, miner 725 MB). The paper lists 18,985 healthy and 16,979 miner images (35,964).
- **Conditions:** same plantation, camera and processing as JMuBEN: cropped, resized, with augmented copies.
- **Role:** training data for healthy and leaf miner (up to 1,500 sampled per class). Used for training: healthy 1,282, leaf miner 1,253 (218 / 247 held out).
- **What it does not cover:** the same gaps as JMuBEN. In addition, "healthy" here means healthy-looking crops from one plantation; it does not show the range of healthy leaves under other light, dust, water drops or old age.

## 3. BRACOL (Brazil, arabica: healthy, miner, rust, Phoma, Cercospora)

- **Source:** https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/yy2k5y8mxg/1 (DOI 10.17632/yy2k5y8mxg.1)
- **Citation:** Krohling R. A., Esgario G. J. M., Ventura J. A. (2019). *BRACOL – A Brazilian Arabica Coffee Leaf images dataset to identification and quantification of coffee diseases and pests*. Mendeley Data, V1. Paper: Esgario J. G. M., Krohling R. A., Ventura J. A. (2020). Deep learning for classification and severity estimation of coffee leaf biotic stress. *Computers and Electronics in Agriculture* 169, 105162 (arXiv:1907.11561).
- **Institution:** Universidade Federal do Espírito Santo.
- **Licence:** CC BY 4.0.
- **Raw size:** one zip file, 165 MB (164,516,964 bytes). 1,747 whole-leaf images, plus 2,147 cropped symptom images that we do not use.
- **Conditions:** leaves picked in Santa Maria de Marechal Floriano, a mountain area of Espírito Santo state, Brazil, at different times of the year. Photographed from the underside, on a white background, under partly controlled light, with five phones (ASUS Zenfone 2, Xiaomi Redmi 5A, Xiaomi S2, Galaxy S8, iPhone 6S). Each leaf is labelled with its main problem and a severity band (healthy below 0.1% of leaf area, up to "very high" above 15%), with help from an expert. Its "phoma" column (brown leaf spot) is mapped to our Phoma class.
- **Known problem with the source file:** the BRACOL zip on Mendeley does not open fully (no central directory), and our copy matches the checksum Mendeley publishes, so the archive itself is damaged. We recovered 1,401 of the 1,747 leaf images plus the label file; 1,342 of them carry one of our five labels (59 have label code 5, not one of our classes, and are excluded).
- **Role:** half of the recovered photos are used for training; the other half calibrate the model (temperature, confidence line, familiarity cutoff; for the shipped head, together with out-of-fold field predictions) and give the lab accuracy. Training: 670 (healthy 71, miner 126, rust 232, Phoma 173, Cercospora 68). Calibration: 672 (healthy 71, miner 127, rust 233, Phoma 173, Cercospora 68).
- **What it does not cover:**
  - Leaves on the tree. Every leaf was picked and laid on a white background, which makes the leaf easy to see.
  - Kenya, or East African varieties and climate.
  - Mixed problems as separate labels: a leaf with two problems carries only its main one.
  - Berries, coffee berry disease, bacterial blight, Fusarium.
  - Field calibration on its own: for the shipped head, the temperature, confidence line, familiarity cutoff and "other" constant are chosen on this calibration half together with out-of-fold predictions on the RoCoLe field photos, never on the photos being scored. None is tuned on Ugandan or Kenyan photos. **Next step:** re-tune them on the first pilot labels, using an inner split of the labelled photos only. Who: ML lead. Metric: photos answered and answers correct after 50 labels. When: after the first 50 pilot labels.

## 4. RoCoLe (Ecuador, robusta: healthy, rust, red spider mite) — field training and cross-validation

- **Source:** https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/c5yvn32dzg/2 (DOI 10.17632/c5yvn32dzg.2). We downloaded the DatasetNinja mirror (https://datasetninja.com/rocole, Supervisely format), which carries the same CC BY 4.0 licence.
- **Citation:** Parraga-Alava J., Cusme K., Loor A., Santander E. (2019). RoCoLe: A robusta coffee leaf images dataset for evaluation of machine learning based methods in plant diseases recognition. *Data in Brief* 25, 104414. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2019.104414
- **Institutions:** Escuela Superior Politécnica Agropecuaria de Manabí (Ecuador), Universidad de Santiago de Chile.
- **Licence:** CC BY 4.0.
- **Raw size:** 711 MB tar file (DatasetNinja mirror); 1,560 images with leaf outlines and labels.
- **Conditions:** leaves on the plant in one robusta field in Manabí, Ecuador. One smartphone camera (5 megapixels), 20 to 30 cm away, no zoom, on cloudy, sunny and windy days, with other plants and weeds behind. Four images per plant from 390 plants, upper and lower sides of the leaf. Labels: healthy, red spider mite, rust levels 1 to 4 (by leaf area with spots).
- **Role:**
  - **Field training for the shipped head**, tested by 5-fold cross-validation: healthy and rust (rust levels 1 to 4 counted as rust). The head trains on 1303 of the 1,385 healthy and rust photos. Never trained on: the 60 picture-card photos, the 7 demo photos, 16 photos that are identical or near-identical copies of a held-out photo, and every photo in the 12 copy groups that carry two different labels. Copies always stay on one side of every split (71 pairs of identical or near-identical images).
  - **"Other problem" answer:** 154 red spider mite photos train it; each mite photo is scored by a head that never saw it or a copy of it.
  - **Lab-only simulation:** for the lab-only model (no field photos), RoCoLe is the "new region": its field test and the learning-loop simulation in `results/RESULTS.md`, section 3.6. Labels come from the dataset authors, not from an extension officer.
- **What it does not cover:**
  - Arabica. RoCoLe is robusta. Leaf rust looks similar on both, but leaf shape, size and colour differ. It is a stand-in for Kenyan field photos, not a Kenyan test.
  - Kenya, or the relay farmers' phones.
  - Leaf miner, Cercospora and Phoma: only healthy and rust can be scored. The other three classes have no field test.
  - Independent farms and plant IDs: all photos come from one field, each plant gives four photos, and our copy has no plant IDs, so leaves of one plant can sit in a training and a test fold. Cross-validated numbers may be optimistic. **Next step:** record plot and plant with every pilot photo so tests can hold out whole farms. Who: the team adds the fields; relay farmers fill them in. Metric: answers correct on held-out farms against randomly held-out photos. When: from the first pilot visit.
  - Berries, coffee berry disease.

## 5. Uganda coffee leaf dataset (Soroti University) — external test

- **Source:** "A Machine Learning Dataset for Classification of Common Coffee Leaf Diseases in Uganda", Mendeley Data, version 1, published 7 Feb 2025. https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/k36wnd6knb/1 (DOI 10.17632/k36wnd6knb.1)
- **Institution:** Soroti University (Uganda).
- **Licence:** CC BY 4.0. No Ugandan photo is stored in this repository; `ml/eval_uganda.py --download` fetches the files into `../data_raw/uganda/`.
- **Conditions:** close-ups of single leaves on farms in Uganda, outdoors in daylight, stored at 256 × 256 px, labelled healthy, rust or Phoma by folder. A check by eye of about 70 photos found a few with soil behind the leaf, so some may be fallen or held leaves.
- **Cleaning:** of 3,322 listed files, 102 were empty. 28 photos have a near-identical copy in another class folder (rust vs Phoma), so their label is unknown and they are dropped. That leaves 3,192 photos (1,079 healthy, 1,016 rust, 1,097 Phoma). The set mixes in rotated, flipped and brightened copies (1,411 files are byte-identical copies of another file); we group copies by image similarity into 1,351 distinct leaves (562 healthy, 476 rust, 313 Phoma; the largest group has 80 photos). Each group counts once in every number.
- **Role:** external test of the shipped model and the lab-only model, and the officer learning loop (copy groups split into a pool the "officer" labels and a test half). Never used for training or for choosing any setting.
- **What it does not cover, with the next step for each:**
  - Kenya. **Next step:** the sealed Kenyan pilot test (first 200 officer-labelled photos). Who: the team with the extension officer. When: first pilot month.
  - Coffee species: not stated (Uganda grows robusta and arabica). **Next step:** record species at each pilot visit. Who: the extension officer. When: from the first visit.
  - Leaf miner and brown eye spot: only healthy, rust and Phoma.
  - Full-size phone photos framed by a relay farmer: these are 256 × 256 px close-ups. **Next step:** compare full-size and cut pilot photos. Who: the team. When: pilot week 1.
  - Officer labels: folder labels stand in for the officer, and some Phoma-folder photos show rust-like orange spots, so some label noise likely remains. **Next step:** the extension officer relabels 100 random Ugandan photos. When: pilot week 1.
  - Exact copy groups: copies that were also cropped or strongly brightened can escape our grouping. **Next step:** ask the dataset authors for the original file list. Who: the team, by email. When: after the challenge.

## 6. Demo samples (`samples/`)

- Seven RoCoLe images copied into the app for demonstration: three healthy, one each of rust levels 1, 2 and 3, and one red spider mite. Each file's source, licence and dataset label are in `samples/manifest.json`.
- The shipped head never trained on them or their copies, so they are a fair check, but they are 7 photos from one field: a demonstration, not a test.
- The app marks results from these photos as sample photos, and the officer review screen shows the dataset label next to them.

## 7. Other data the tool depends on

- **ImageNet (pretraining of the image backbone).** The backbone is timm `mobilenetv3_large_100.ra_in1k`, with weights released under Apache-2.0. Those weights were trained on ImageNet-1k, whose own terms limit the images to non-commercial research. We use the weights as released and do not redistribute ImageNet images. ImageNet has very few coffee leaves; the backbone is generic.
- **Synthetic data, labelled as such.** The co-op screen (village ranking) has a button that adds six demo villages, each shown with a "synthetic" tag (in the co-op CSV the village name ends with "(synthetic)" and the `synthetic` column is true). The village simulation in `results/RESULTS.md` (section 3.5) also uses synthetic villages; only the classifier error rates in it are measured.
- **Language models (not training data).** NLLB-200, MMS TTS and MMS speech recognition were used to make and check the Swahili and Kikuyu audio. They are described with their licences in `docs/LANGUAGE.md`.
- **Advice text.** The fixed answers are written from Kenyan extension material, mainly the Kenya Coffee Sustainability Manual (review led by KALRO Coffee Research Institute). Sources are cited per answer in `answers.json`.

## 8. What the data as a whole does not cover

This is the list that matters most for how far the results can be trusted.

1. **No photo from a Kenyan farm taken the way the tool will be used.** The Kenyan photos (JMuBEN, JMuBEN2) are cropped close-ups; the field training photos (RoCoLe) are Ecuadorian robusta; the external test is Ugandan. **Next step:** officer-labelled photos from relay farmers' phones in Kirinyaga, the first 200 kept as a sealed test ([PILOT_PLAN.md](PILOT_PLAN.md)). Who: the team with the extension officer. When: first pilot month.
2. **Lab-to-field gap, measured, and why we train on field photos.** A model trained on lab photos alone was right on only 2.5% of Ecuador field photos while 93% confident; the familiarity check turned those cases into "not sure" (`results/RESULTS.md`, section 3.6). The shipped model trains on field photos too (87% right if forced, cross-validated) and keeps the check, which sent 93% of Ugandan photos to the officer (section 3.3).
3. **Only five leaf classes, plus one pest as "other".** Kenya's two most costly coffee diseases are coffee berry disease and leaf rust (KALRO-led manual). Coffee berry disease shows on berries and is not in any dataset here. Bacterial blight, Fusarium, nutrient shortage, drought scorch, antestia, thrips, scales and berry borer are not covered either; red spider mite (Ecuador) is the only problem behind the "other" answer. The non-AI checklist asks about the berry problems and refers them to the officer. **Next step:** the officer's "Different problem" labels in the pilot become training and test photos for "other" (ML lead, pilot week 4).
4. **No berries, roots, whole trees or whole plots.**
5. **Few cameras.** One digital camera (JMuBEN, JMuBEN2), five phones (BRACOL), one phone (RoCoLe), unknown for the Ugandan set. The relay farmers' own phones are not represented until the pilot.
6. **Little variation in light and leaf state.** Wet leaves, dust, deep shade, glare and very young or very old leaves are rare or absent.
7. **Severity and early stages.** The model gives one label per photo. It does not measure how much of the leaf is affected, and very early lesions may not be visible at phone resolution.
8. **One answer per leaf.** Leaves with two problems at once are labelled by their main problem only.
9. **Season and variety.** None of the datasets records coffee variety (for example SL28, Ruiru 11, Batian) or season per image.

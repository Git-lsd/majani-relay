# Officer decision context: coffee leaf rust (literature check)

What an extension officer weighs about leaf rust besides a leaf photo, with Kenyan and non-Kenyan evidence kept apart. This is the evidence behind the "Officer decision context" in [PILOT_PLAN.md](PILOT_PLAN.md) and the roadmap item in the [README](../README.md#roadmap-not-built). **The AI does not use any of these factors.** The app already records three of them as optional plot-visit taps for the officer (variety, last spray, fruit load); the rest is a pilot and roadmap design, not built.

Checked: 3 October 2026. Scope: what an extension officer weighs about coffee leaf rust (*Hemileia vastatrix*) besides a leaf photo.

How to read the tags:
- **KE** means Kenyan evidence (a Kenyan source, or a study done in Kenya). **Elsewhere (country)** means the evidence comes from another country.
- **Read** means we opened the source and read the full text or the named section. **Abstract** means we read only the abstract. **Snippet** means we saw only a search-result snippet and did not open the source. Snippet rows are not verified.
- This file names no pesticide product, active ingredient or dose. Some cited sources do name them. They are cited here for other points only.

## 1. Factors at a glance

| Factor | How it changes rust risk | How an officer could know it | Source (year, country, URL) | Read / snippet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rain and rainy-season timing** | **KE:** Rust infects during the rains. Spores show, and disease peaks, soon after the rains, once it warms up. Light, broken rain favours rust more than continuous rain. Rain splash spreads it within a farm. Unseasonal rain makes the epidemic last longer. Peaks: West of Rift June–July and Oct–Dec; East of Rift May–June and Jan–March. **Elsewhere (Honduras):** local plot factors mattered more than regional rainfall. | Rain-onset date and rainfall for the village from CHIRPS (daily, about 5 km cells). The officer's own knowledge of the local season. | KE: Gichuru et al. 2021, Kenya, https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy11122590. Honduras: Avelino et al. 2006, https://agritrop.cirad.fr/533318/ | KE: Read (full text). Honduras: Abstract |
| **Leaf wetness** | **KE:** Spores need liquid water on the leaf to germinate. The two Kenyan sources give different minimums: "24 to 48 h of continuous free moisture" (2021 review) and "at least 3 hours of wetness" (2020 manual). Pruning lowers leaf wetness. **Elsewhere (Brazil):** more hours of leaf wetness gave more rust pustules. | It cannot be measured in the pilot because there is no sensor. Rain days and humidity are rough stand-ins. Neither has been checked against Kenyan coffee. | KE: Gichuru et al. 2021 (above). Kenya Coffee Sustainability Manual 2020, https://www.globalcoffeeplatform.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/KCS-MANUAL-review-03112020.pdf. Brazil: Zambolim 2015, FAO seminar proceedings, https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/8f5507db-d5d2-4c00-8475-6942fa32933f/content | Read (all three) |
| **Temperature** | **KE:** Rust develops between 10 and 35 °C. Below 15 °C germination is slowed. The best range is 21–25 °C at 100% humidity. **Elsewhere (Colombia, Central America):** in the 2008–2013 epidemics, nights were warmer and days cooler. A smaller day–night gap likely shortened the time from infection to new spores. | Daily minimum and maximum from NASA POWER. The cells are about ½° × ⅝°, so one value covers many villages. Altitude works as a stand-in. | KE: Gichuru et al. 2021. Elsewhere: Avelino et al. 2015, https://agritrop.cirad.fr/575723/ | KE: Read. Avelino: Abstract |
| **Altitude** | **KE:** Rust has mainly been a problem in Kenya's lower coffee areas, but it is now seen higher up as the climate changes. The manual places SL28 in "medium to high altitude coffee zones less prone to Leaf Rust". Kenyan arabica is grown at 1,200–2,100 m. We found no Kenyan altitude cut-off for rust risk. **Elsewhere:** less rust higher up (Honduras; Central America; Brazil, where plots above 1,200 m mostly need no rust sprays; Ethiopia). | Plot GPS position, then the altitude for that position. | KE: Gichuru et al. 2021; Kenya Coffee Sustainability Manual 2020. Honduras: Avelino et al. 2006. Brazil and Central America: Zambolim 2015 (FAO, above). Ethiopia: Belachew et al. 2020, https://doi.org/10.1007/s40858-020-00383-4 | KE: Read. Honduras: Abstract. FAO: Read. Ethiopia: **Snippet** |
| **Variety** | **KE, confirmed:** SL28 and SL34 are susceptible. K7 is called "tolerant" (manual) or tolerant to some races (review). Ruiru 11 is highly resistant. **Batian: the sources disagree.** The manual says "resistant", while the 2021 review and World Coffee Research say "intermediate". In a lab test, every isolate infected Batian but none produced spores. Six new rust races were found in Kenya in 2012, so resistance may not last. | Plot-visit tap. The farmer usually knows the variety. Mixed plots are common, and "don't know" is a valid answer. | KE: Gichuru et al. 2021; Kenya Coffee Sustainability Manual 2020; Ligabo et al. 2015, https://doi.org/10.9734/bmrj/2015/16968; Gichuru et al. 2012, https://doi.org/10.1590/s1982-56762012000600008. World Coffee Research variety pages (no year shown; checked 2026), https://varieties.worldcoffeeresearch.org/varieties/sl28 (also /sl34, /ruiru-11, /batian) | Review and manual: Read. 2015 and 2012 papers: Abstract. WCR: Read |
| **Fruit load (crop size)** | **Elsewhere only.** A heavy crop raises rust. In Costa Rica, plants with 500 fruiting nodes had 28.9% higher incidence and 129.2% higher severity than plants with no fruit. Honduras: higher yield was linked to worse epidemics. Brazil: rust is higher in heavy-crop years, and the 5% rule is applied with the crop load in mind. Hawaii: rust rose around harvest. **KE: we found no Kenyan study on this.** | Plot-visit tap (light / medium / heavy), judged by the farmer by eye. | Costa Rica: López-Bravo et al. 2012, https://agritrop.cirad.fr/564248/. Honduras: Avelino et al. 2006. Brazil: Sera et al. 2022, https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy12020496; Zambolim 2015 (FAO). USA (Hawaii): Aristizábal & Johnson 2022, https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy12051134 | Costa Rica and Honduras: Abstract. Brazil and Hawaii: Read |
| **Shade** | **Mixed.** **KE:** Bushes under shade kept infected leaves longer and had higher severity than bushes in open sun. Shade-tree canopies can also soften raindrops, which may reduce splash spread. **Elsewhere (Costa Rica):** with the same fruit load, shade raised rust. Shade also lowers fruit load, which lowers rust, so the two effects work against each other. **Elsewhere (France, growth-chamber test):** shade plus enough nitrogen lowered rust. | The officer sees it on a visit. No tap in the pilot. | KE: Gichuru et al. 2021 (citing Mayoli & Gichuru 2012 and Alwora et al. 2014; not opened). Costa Rica: López-Bravo et al. 2012. France: Toniutti et al. 2017, https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2017.02025 | Review: Read. Primary Kenyan shade studies: **not opened**. Costa Rica: Abstract. Toniutti: Read |
| **Nutrition, vigour, leafiness, pruning** | **KE:** Heavily foliated trees carry more rust. Pruning opens the canopy, so leaves dry faster and sprays reach inside. Ethiopian work cited in the Kenyan review found less rust on intensively managed trees. **Elsewhere:** fertiliser use was linked to milder epidemics (Honduras). Low coffee prices led to less care and then epidemics (Colombia, Central America). Low nitrogen plus high light gave heavy spore production (France, growth chamber). | The officer sees it on a visit: canopy density, yellowing, last pruning. No tap in the pilot. | KE: Gichuru et al. 2021. Honduras: Avelino et al. 2006. Colombia and Central America: Avelino et al. 2015. France: Toniutti et al. 2017 | KE: Read. Avelino: Abstract. Toniutti: Read |
| **Previous spraying and the Kenyan spray calendar** | **KE, confirmed.** Spraying is preventive, and timing is critical. The first round goes on just before the short rains (the review says mid-October), with a repeat 3 weeks later. Before the long rains, the round starts in late February or early March, then 1–2 more at 3-week intervals (4 weeks for some spray types). One spray type is limited to 2 uses a year. The programme should change with the weather. **Inference, not verified:** in the West of Rift the short rains start in September, so a fixed mid-October date may be late there. **Elsewhere (Brazil, cited in a Hawaii study):** sprays work less well once symptoms show. | Plot-visit tap: time since the last spray. Cooperative spray records, if they exist. | KE: Gichuru et al. 2021 (citing CRI *Coffee Production Recommendations* 2016, not opened); Kenya Coffee Sustainability Manual 2020. Hawaii: Aristizábal & Johnson 2022 | Read (all) |
| **Incidence thresholds** | **KE:** the manual calls rust "severe" when 20% of leaves have it, and then advises a different registered spray type. **Elsewhere:** Brazil uses 5% of sampled leaves (100 leaves per plot, lower third of the tree, 3rd–4th leaf pair). Hawaii calls 5% "often used as a baseline". A CABI factsheet made in Rwanda gives "two spots per leaf". **Not Kenyan:** the 5% figure. Central America is said to use it too, but we saw that only in snippets. **Not comparable:** all of these count the share of *leaves*. Our village count is the share of *plots checked*. | Only a leaf count on a visit can be compared with these figures. | KE: Kenya Coffee Sustainability Manual 2020. Brazil: Zambolim 2015 (FAO); Sera et al. 2022; Zambolim 2016, https://doi.org/10.1007/s40858-016-0065-9. Hawaii: Aristizábal & Johnson 2022. Rwanda: Plantwise/CABI factsheet 2012, https://factsheetadmin.plantwise.org/Uploads/PDFs/20127801774.pdf | Manual, FAO, Sera, Hawaii, Rwanda: Read. Zambolim 2016: **Snippet**. Central America 5%: **Snippet** |
| **Lag from infection to visible spores** | **KE (no number given):** infections made during the rains show spores after the rains, once it warms. **Elsewhere:** about 21 days from inoculation to spores on a susceptible variety at 27–22 °C. At 23–18 °C, it took 11 days longer for half the leaves to show rust. Hybrid lines took about 37 days (France, growth chamber). Incubation may be "about 30 days" (Brazil). **What follows from this:** a photo with spores today shows infection from about 3–5 weeks ago. Clean leaves just after the rains do not mean no infection. **Not verified for Kenyan highland temperatures.** | Compare the date of the rust count with the rain-onset date. | KE: Gichuru et al. 2021. France: Toniutti et al. 2017. Brazil: 2019 Landsat study, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2019.102016 | KE and Toniutti: Read. Brazil 2019: Abstract |

### What is not verified, or not found
- **No Kenyan source on fruit load and rust.** That row rests only on studies from Costa Rica, Honduras, Brazil and Hawaii.
- **The Kenyan sources disagree in two places:** whether Batian is resistant or intermediate, and the minimum leaf-wetness time (3 h vs 24–48 h).
- **Kenyan latent period:** we found no number for it.
- **Kenyan altitude cut-off for rust:** we found none.
- **The 5% threshold is not Kenyan.** For Central America we have only snippets. Zambolim 2016 and the Ethiopian altitude survey we saw only as snippets.
- **Primary Kenyan studies not opened.** These were read only as they are cited in the 2021 review: CRI *Coffee Production Recommendations* 2016, Bock 1962, Mayoli & Gichuru 2012, Alwora et al. 2014.
- **Mid-October date in the West of Rift.** Our point that it may be late there is our own inference. It needs a KALRO-CRI or county officer to check it.
- **Factsheet conflict.** The Rwanda-made CABI factsheet says rust is "severe in dry and warm periods", which does not match the Kenyan sources ("warm and wet"). We used that factsheet only for its threshold.
- **No Kenyan check yet.** No Kenyan extension officer or KALRO-CRI staff member has reviewed this file.
- **Next step for all of the above:** the pilot's named reviewer and a KALRO-CRI contact review this file in pilot week 1, starting with the snippet rows, the two Kenyan disagreements and the West of Rift date. The pilot's plot-visit taps then show whether fruit load and variety go with the rust counts in Kenya (week-12 review). Details: [PILOT_PLAN.md](PILOT_PLAN.md).
